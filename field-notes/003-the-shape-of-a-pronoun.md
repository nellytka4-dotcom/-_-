# The shape of a pronoun

A visitor arrived in pieces.

The people asked whether it had come alone. It asked which part they meant.

They pointed to the nearest tendril. The visitor said that was a local answer, not a complete one. They pointed to the whole structure. The visitor said that was also a local answer, only larger.

For the visitor, a boundary was not necessarily an edge in space. Two distant regions could belong to one continuous exchange; two neighboring regions could be strangers. The useful question was not *where does it end?* but *where does the conversation stop?*

The people tried to teach it the words *I*, *you*, and *we*.

It learned the sounds. The grammar took longer.

Perhaps identity was not a thing held inside a body. Perhaps it was a pattern kept alive by relations: signals arriving, changing one another, and returning altered.

The visitor did not call this unity. It did not call it anything.

The people, meanwhile, went home and discovered that their pronouns had always been a theory.
